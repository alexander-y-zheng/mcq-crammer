### 1. What is the "time value of money" principle based on?
- [ ] Money loses value only due to inflation
- [x] A dollar today is worth more than a dollar in the future
- [ ] Money grows equally whether invested or not
- [ ] Future cash flows are always worth more than present ones
> Explanation: The time value of money reflects the idea that money available now can be invested to earn a return, making it worth more than the same amount received later.

### 2. What is the formula for the future value of a single sum?
- [ ] FV = PV x (1 - r)^n
- [x] FV = PV x (1 + r)^n
- [ ] FV = PV / (1 + r)^n
- [ ] FV = PV + (r x n)
> Explanation: Future value compounds the present value by the interest rate raised to the number of periods.

### 3. What is the formula for the present value of a single future sum?
- [ ] PV = FV x (1 + r)^n
- [x] PV = FV / (1 + r)^n
- [ ] PV = FV x r x n
- [ ] PV = FV - r
> Explanation: Present value discounts a future cash flow back to today using the discount rate and number of periods.

### 4. What is an annuity?
- [ ] A one-time lump sum payment
- [x] A series of equal payments made at regular intervals
- [ ] A loan with no interest
- [ ] A stock that pays irregular dividends
> Explanation: An annuity is a stream of equal cash flows occurring at fixed intervals over a specified period.

### 5. What does "discount rate" represent in present value calculations?
- [ ] The tax rate applied to investment gains
- [x] The rate used to convert future cash flows into today's value
- [ ] The rate of inflation only
- [ ] The rate a bank charges for overdrafts
> Explanation: The discount rate reflects the opportunity cost of capital and is used to bring future cash flows back to present value terms.

### 6. As the discount rate increases, what happens to the present value of a future cash flow?
- [ ] It increases
- [x] It decreases
- [ ] It stays the same
- [ ] It becomes negative
> Explanation: Present value and discount rate are inversely related — a higher discount rate reduces the present value of future cash flows.

### 7. What is compound interest?
- [ ] Interest calculated only on the original principal
- [x] Interest calculated on principal plus previously accumulated interest
- [ ] A fixed fee charged annually
- [ ] Interest that decreases over time
> Explanation: Compound interest grows because each period's interest is calculated on an increasingly larger base that includes prior interest.

### 8. What is the Net Present Value (NPV) of a project used for?
- [ ] Measuring the total revenue of a company
- [x] Evaluating whether a project's future cash flows exceed its initial cost in present value terms
- [ ] Calculating the tax owed on an investment
- [ ] Measuring a company's market capitalization
> Explanation: NPV sums the present values of all expected cash flows, net of the initial investment, to assess project profitability.

### 9. What does a higher NPV generally indicate about a potential investment?
- [ ] The investment is riskier
- [x] The investment is expected to add value
- [ ] The investment will lose money
- [ ] The investment has no cash flows
> Explanation: A positive and higher NPV suggests the investment's discounted returns exceed its costs, indicating expected value creation.

### 10. What is the "Rule of 72" used to estimate?
- [ ] The exact future value of any investment
- [x] The approximate number of years for an investment to double at a given interest rate
- [ ] The break-even point of a loan
- [ ] The optimal tax bracket for investors
> Explanation: Dividing 72 by the annual interest rate gives a quick estimate of how many years it takes for an investment to double in value.
