### 1. How many signs are in the Western zodiac?
- [ ] 10
- [x] 12
- [ ] 13
- [ ] 7
> Explanation: The Western zodiac has 12 signs, each traditionally spanning about 30 degrees of the sun's apparent path — conveniently ignoring the 13th constellation, Ophiuchus, that astronomers actually pass through.

### 2. If you were born between July 23 and August 22, your sun sign is:
- [ ] Cancer, the sign of people who never leave their childhood bedroom
- [x] Leo, the sign famous for main-character energy
- [ ] Virgo, the sign that alphabetizes its spice rack
- [ ] Libra, the sign that can't pick a restaurant
> Explanation: Leos are astrologically associated with confidence, drama, and warmth — fittingly, since Leo's ruling planet is the Sun, the star everything else in the solar system revolves around.

### 3. What is a "retrograde" in astronomy, the phenomenon astrology loves to blame for chaos?
- [ ] A planet literally moving backward through space
- [x] An optical illusion where a planet appears to move backward from Earth's viewpoint
- [ ] A planet slowing down until it stops
- [ ] A planet switching orbits with another planet
> Explanation: Mercury retrograde is a real astronomical event, but it's just an illusion caused by Earth "lapping" a slower-orbiting planet — no texts are actually being cursed by the universe.

### 4. Which element is Scorpio traditionally associated with?
- [ ] Fire
- [x] Water
- [ ] Earth
- [ ] Air
> Explanation: Scorpio is a water sign, said to bring emotional depth and intensity — or, as skeptics might say, main character syndrome with extra eyeliner.

### 5. What is your "rising sign" (ascendant) based on?
- [ ] Your birth month only
- [x] The zodiac sign on the eastern horizon at your exact time of birth
- [ ] The sign of your favorite celebrity
- [ ] The moon phase during your birth week
> Explanation: The rising sign is calculated from your precise birth time and location, since the horizon's zodiac position shifts roughly every two hours as Earth rotates.

### 6. Which of these is NOT one of the three signs traditionally forming a "fire trine"?
- [ ] Aries
- [ ] Leo
- [ ] Sagittarius
- [x] Capricorn
> Explanation: Aries, Leo, and Sagittarius make up the fire trine, prized for passion and impulsiveness; Capricorn is an earth sign, too busy building a five-year plan to join the fire drama.

### 7. Why do astrologers place such emphasis on the exact date/time of birth?
- [ ] Because it determines your blood type
- [x] Because it determines the precise positions of the sun, moon, and planets used in a birth chart
- [ ] Because hospitals require it for zodiac registration
- [ ] It doesn't actually matter to astrologers
> Explanation: A birth chart plots where celestial bodies were positioned in the sky at your exact birth moment — real astronomical calculations, even if their predictive meaning isn't scientifically supported.

### 8. What does modern science say about the predictive validity of sun-sign astrology?
- [ ] It has been rigorously proven in controlled studies
- [x] Scientific studies have found no reliable evidence that zodiac signs predict personality or life events
- [ ] It works, but only for even-numbered years
- [ ] NASA officially endorses it
> Explanation: Despite astrology's cultural popularity, peer-reviewed research consistently finds no statistically significant link between zodiac signs and personality traits or outcomes.

### 9. Which sign is famously stereotyped as being unable to commit to weekend plans until the very last minute?
- [ ] Taurus, allegedly too stubborn to change plans
- [x] Gemini, allegedly too indecisive to make plans
- [ ] Capricorn, allegedly too busy working to have plans
- [ ] Pisces, allegedly too dreamy to remember plans
> Explanation: Gemini's dual-twin symbolism gets blamed in pop astrology for a reputation of indecisiveness — though in fairness, everyone has that one friend who cancels brunch three times.

### 10. Why did the zodiac constellations shift relative to the calendar dates over the centuries?
- [ ] They didn't; the dates are exact and unchanging
- [x] Because of "precession," the slow wobble of Earth's rotational axis over roughly 26,000 years
- [ ] Because ancient astrologers miscounted the days
- [ ] Because the sun moved to a new galaxy
> Explanation: Earth's axis wobbles slowly over millennia, a real phenomenon called precession, which means the constellations backdrop has drifted since the zodiac was first mapped — so your "sign" isn't quite where the ancients pinned it anymore.
